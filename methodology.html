Method

How this was built, what "verified" means here, and what the site deliberately refuses to do.

The question being answered

Two adults, seven nights in New York, somewhere between 15 February and 23 March 2027, preferring 15–18 February. Rank hotels by price, location and amenities with a queen bed and private bathroom. Show the hotel cost as a 7-night total and identify the cheapest booking window. Pull the Michelin picture — and keep "starred" separate from "listed". Rank hotels by how many starred restaurants are reachable. Give a cost for two at each restaurant. Attach hours, closures, an official link and a video to every row.

The verification rule

A value is verified when it was read, character for character, off a page belonging to the venue, the agency that runs it, or the Michelin Guide — and that page's URL is recorded next to it. A value is unverified when no such page printed it. Unverified values are blank, tagged unverified, and listed in the questions register. Nothing on this site is inferred, estimated, recalled or plausibility-checked into existence.

What that rule cost

  • Eleven Madison Park and Gramercy Tavern have no hours on this site, despite both being easy to guess.
  • Per Se and Atera have no price. The famous "$390" and "$365" figures circulating online are not on either official page.
  • The Met has no hours, because its own page would not render them across three URL attempts.
  • Refinery has no 7-night total, because its page shows only nightly rates.

How the transit figures were made

Origin and destination coordinates were geocoded with OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, house number only — asking it for "The Maritime Hotel 363 West 16th Street New York" returns nothing, while "363 West 16th Street New York" resolves. Each pair was then sent to Transitous, an open routing service backed by MTA NYC Transit GTFS and OSM, at a weekday midday departure, and the returned door-to-door duration and transfer count were recorded verbatim.

The batch isochrone endpoint failed on every attempt, so no travel-time matrix exists. The transit page publishes the nine measured legs and labels the cluster screen as a straight-line approximation, not a travel time.

Site structure

  • index.html — executive summary, KPI grid, 7-day shape, budget
  • hotels.html — ten rooms, seasonality test, per-hotel detail
  • restaurants.html — starred table, menu detail, star-vs-listing separation
  • activities.html — six verified attractions plus an unverified shortlist
  • transit.html — measured legs, cluster screen, fares
  • questions.html — the unknowns register and the decisions they imply
  • suggestions.html — ten recommendations, kept separate from the data
  • review.html — sources, irregularities, manual-review checklist
  • data/*.json — the single source of truth; build.py renders every page from it

Mobile & accessibility

  • Single stylesheet, no framework, no external fonts or scripts — the whole site loads in one round trip.
  • Every table sits in a horizontally scrollable wrapper with a sticky header row, so wide tables stay usable at 360 px.
  • Body text is 16 px with a 1.55 line height; the layout collapses to a single column below 640 px.
  • Links and status tags all carry text, never colour alone.

Rebuilding

python3 build.py

Reads data/hotels.json, data/michelin.json, data/restaurants.json, data/activities.json and data/transit.json; writes the HTML pages at the repo root. No dependencies beyond the Python 3 standard library.
