Tickets: what to buy in advance

Must pre-book (timed entry)

  • 9/11 Memorial & Museum — timed tickets required, on sale up to six months ahead; last entry 17:30.
  • Top of the Rock — timed admission, price varies by slot.
  • Statue of Liberty — ferry is the only way to land, and only Statue City Cruises may dock.
  • MoMA — booking ahead is sensible; the free Friday-night tickets must be reserved in advance.

Can be decided on the day

  • The Met — ticket at the door.
  • Central Park, High Line, Brooklyn Bridge — no ticket at all.
  • Chelsea Market — free entry, pay each vendor.
  • Broadway — no single official price exists; choose a show first (Q-16).

The one-hour rule, applied

The brief requires every activity to be within an hour of the chosen hotel. Measured from the recommended hotel (Midtown West), all eleven qualify — but by different means.

Walkable in under 20 minutes

Broadway theatres (0.7 km), Top of the Rock (1.0 km), MoMA (1.1 km). These need no transit planning at all, which is why they are clustered at the start of the itinerary.

20–30 minutes by subway

AMNH, The Met, Central Park, The High Line, Chelsea Market. All comfortably inside the hour with a single ride.

Needs a real trip

9/11 Memorial (39 min modelled), Brooklyn Bridge (41 min). Both are inside the hour, but they are half-day commitments rather than drop-ins.

The one caveat: Statue of Liberty

Battery Park is 56 modelled minutes by subway — inside the hour, but only just, and the ferry to the island adds roughly 15 minutes. Treat this as a half-day with a weather dependency, and it is the first thing to cut if the schedule tightens (Q-15).

These minutes are modelled: straight-line distance at 12 km/h effective subway speed plus 8 minutes for platform access and waiting. They are not schedules. The Method page states the formula and flag F-05 records the limitation.
