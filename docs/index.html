Executive summary

1 · The decision

Book Fairfield Inn & Suites by Marriott New York Manhattan/Central Park, King Room, for 15–22 February 2027 at $1,155.48 including taxes and fees, on the free-cancellation rate (cancel free until 12 Feb 2027). Once tax is included it is the cheapest of the seven, it includes breakfast for two, the bed is a king and the bathroom is attached.

Six of the seven advertise a total that excludes 14.75% tax and a $4/night city tax, so their headline numbers flatter them. Fairfield's headline already includes everything. On list price before discounts Arlo SoHo advertised $1,639.00 against Fairfield's $1,640.18, so Fairfield would lose on list price — the ranking here is driven by the discounted all-in figure, and the discount is disclosed on the Hotels page rather than absorbed (F-03).

2 · Why 7 nights, not 3

The brief's preferred window is 15–18 February (3 nights) but it also asks for a 1–2 week trip. Those two statements cannot both be satisfied, so the plan uses 7 nights and keeps a 4-night variant and a 14-night variant on the Itinerary page as choices. No re-planning is needed to switch.

15 February 2027 is the third Monday of February — the US Presidents' Day holiday — so expect holiday demand and thinner restaurant availability.

3 · The restaurants

MICHELIN's 18 November 2025 ceremony lists 72 star entries: 5 three-star, 15 two-star and 52 one-star, and 3 of the star holders also carry a Green Star. 63 are in Manhattan, 6 in Brooklyn, 1 in Queens and 2 outside the city (Tarrytown and North Salem — not reachable within the hour).

Of eight priority venues with a detail page, Le Bernardin ($350 pp) and Gabriel Kreuther ($325 pp) publish an exact menu price; Per Se publishes only a $200 deposit floor. Four more are blank rather than guessed. See the table →

4 · The one-hour rule

All seven shortlisted hotels are inside the modelled one-hour reach of the verified star restaurants, so the rule does not separate them — price and breakfast do. That is a model result, not a measurement, and it is labelled as such throughout (flag F-05).

A measured travel-time matrix is the single highest-value improvement available: suggestion S-02.

5 · What is verified, and what is not

  • $4,107.48 verified subtotal across hotel, flights, transit, five tickets and two tasting menus.
  • 7 of 72 restaurants have a full week of verified hours (read on official MICHELIN or venue pages on 25–26 Nov 2026); 1 more is partial and the rest are blank rather than guessed. 2 have an exact published menu price and 1 more publishes only a booking-deposit floor. Every other row is complete on name, distinction, cuisine, borough and official link (see the table).
  • 10 verified official social accounts across restaurants and activities, and 3 verified third-party review videos (all Le Bernardin). One further handle is inferred rather than opened (F-08) and is not counted as verified. Every other social cell is a search link, labelled as a search — TikTok returned HTTP 400 on every read, so no TikTok account is claimed anywhere.
  • 22 flagged items and 26 open questions, each with the exact source that would settle it.

6 · The three things that could invalidate this plan

  1. 14 December 2026: MICHELIN publishes a new New York list. Stars can move before you travel.
  2. Hotel prices are live: every total here is a 25 Nov 2026 snapshot and several depend on a time-limited promotion.
  3. Menu prices are mostly unknown: five priority restaurants publish no readable price, so the food budget is incomplete by design rather than by accident.
